Why journaling comes first

You can't become lucid in dreams you don't remember. A dream journal trains recall: the more you write, the more you notice. Within a couple of weeks many people find their dreams more vivid and easier to recall.

Your bedtime setup

Keep a notebook and pen by the bed. Before sleep, play Lucid Oddysee or New Horizons Astral Experience, two tracks the seller made for lucid dreaming, and repeat a short intention such as “I'll recognise when I'm dreaming.”

The morning entry

Write before you check your phone. Note anything at all: a place, a feeling, a colour. Underline recurring themes. These “dream signs” become cues you can learn to spot while dreaming.

Keep it light

Lucid dreaming is a skill that comes and goes, so be patient. Prioritise good sleep over late-night experiments, and stop if the practice leaves you anxious or tired.
