What “432 Hz” actually means

Most modern music is tuned so the A above middle C vibrates at 440 Hz. 432 Hz music lowers that reference pitch slightly, which shifts every note down a little. To many ears the result sounds a touch warmer and softer. That subjective quality is why meditation and relaxation producers often use it.

What the research says

Studies comparing 432 Hz and 440 Hz music are few and small, so it's best to treat claims about special healing properties with caution. Broader research is stronger. NCCIH's fact sheet on music and health summarises studies where music-based interventions showed promise for anxiety, mood and pain, while noting that many trials are preliminary. In short, listening to calming music you enjoy is reasonable. The specific benefits of 432 Hz are not proven.

Getting the most from 432 Hz tracks

Listen at a moderate volume, in a space where you can relax for at least five minutes. Breathe slowly. Notice how the music feels rather than analysing it. The benefit you're most likely to feel is the simplest one: a few quiet minutes that are just for you.
