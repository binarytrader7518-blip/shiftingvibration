How binaural beats work

A binaural beat happens when each ear hears a slightly different frequency, for example 200 Hz on the left and 206 Hz on the right. Your brain perceives a third, pulsing 6 Hz “beat” that isn't physically in either signal. Because the effect depends on separating the two tones, headphones matter.

What the evidence says

A 2019 meta-analysis in Psychological Research pooled 22 studies and found a medium overall effect of binaural beats on measures of cognition, anxiety and pain perception. Effects were larger with longer exposure and when listening began before a task. Results varied with the frequency used, and the studies differ widely, so it's an encouraging but not definitive picture.

Listening safely

Use comfortable stereo headphones at a moderate volume. The NIDCD notes that loud or prolonged sound can permanently damage hearing, and that this kind of hearing loss is preventable. Don't listen while driving. If you have epilepsy or a seizure disorder, talk to your doctor before using rhythmic audio.
